TEMPORAL DO-OVER RULES

Proposed Safety, Consent, and Non-Interference Framework

PURPOSE AND RESEARCH STATUS

This document establishes proposed principles for evaluating a possible future backward-time-travel intervention, referred to in this archive as a "do-over."

The framework is intended for serious scientific, legal, ethical, and engineering investigation. Its requirements have not been tested as an operational temporal system.

The purpose is to investigate whether a limited personal do-over could ever be considered without creating unacceptable harm to other people, communities, or historical events.

A request for a do-over does not establish that backward time travel is possible or that an intervention should be authorized.

No intervention may proceed without demonstrated scientific feasibility and the required independent approvals.

RULE 1: NON-INTERFERENCE BY DEFAULT

Backward-time intervention must be prohibited by default unless a future, scientifically demonstrated process establishes that a specific intervention can be considered safely and lawfully.

The following principles apply:

No person has an automatic right to alter historical events.

No intervention should proceed merely because an individual requests it.

The protection of other people's lives, rights, safety, and autonomy must be considered.

Any proposed intervention must be evaluated for direct and indirect consequences.

Uncertainty about possible harm must be treated as a reason for caution.

A proposed exception would require scientific evidence, legal authorization, independent ethical review, and appropriate informed consent.

The absence of a detected risk must not be treated as proof that no risk exists.

RULE 2: PROTECTION OF IMPORTANT HISTORICAL EVENTS

Any proposed do-over must be assessed for its potential effects on important historical events and the people connected to them.

The assessment should consider:

Whether the intervention could cause harm to another person.

Whether it could change significant events in ways that create unacceptable consequences.

Whether it could interfere with another person's rights, choices, or opportunities.

Whether the requested change could produce effects beyond the original objective.

Whether the proposed outcome can be assessed with sufficient scientific confidence.

Personal milestones and meaningful life experiences should be treated with care and respect.

However, no event should be declared scientifically or morally untouchable merely through an automated system. Decisions must be based on transparent legal, ethical, and safety standards.

The system must not assume that it can perfectly predict every consequence of changing an event.

RULE 3: LIMITED SCOPE AND PROTECTION OF OTHERS

The original intention of a limited personal do-over is retained.

Any future proposal would need to define its authorized scope before consideration.

The intended scope would be limited to the stated personal objective and the minimum necessary circumstances surrounding it.

However, a personal intervention cannot automatically be assumed to affect only the requester and people immediately nearby.

Possible indirect consequences must also be considered.

The assessment must include:

Directly affected individuals.

People whose circumstances could materially change.

Potential effects on communities and wider historical events.

Privacy and consent requirements.

The possibility of unpredictable consequences.

No private information may be accessed simply because someone appears on a traveler's personal Known List.

The Known List, if used in a future research model, must not override the rights, privacy, or autonomy of its members.

RULE 4: INFORMED CONSENT AND PERSONAL AUTONOMY

Any future research involving human participants must respect informed consent.

The following protections are proposed:

Participants must understand the purpose and potential risks of the research.

Personal information must be limited to the approved purpose.

No person may be subjected to unauthorized psychological, biometric, or neural monitoring.

Consent must not be assumed from silence or previous participation.

Participants must have a defined process for withdrawing consent.

No automated system may independently override a person's legal rights or personal autonomy.

A requester's consent does not substitute for the rights and protections owed to other affected individuals.

RULE 5: SCIENTIFIC AND LEGAL AUTHORIZATION

A do-over must not be treated as an available operation until the underlying technology has been scientifically demonstrated and independently evaluated.

Any future authorization process would require:

Evidence that the proposed temporal process is physically and technically possible.

Independent scientific assessment.

A documented safety and causal-impact evaluation.

Applicable government and legal authorization.

Independent ethical and privacy review.

Appropriate informed consent.

A defined procedure for stopping or suspending an unsafe operation.

No traveler, researcher, government agency, or automated system should have unrestricted authority to authorize an intervention.

RULE 6: PROHIBITED EXPLOITATION

Any future temporal intervention framework must prohibit the use of temporal technology for unfair personal or financial advantage.

Examples include:

Manipulating lottery outcomes.

Obtaining gambling advantages through historical knowledge.

Financial manipulation using information unavailable to others.

Deliberately exploiting or harming other people.

Circumventing legal protections.

Unauthorized interference with protected institutions or events.

These restrictions would apply equally to the original requester and all other participants.

RULE 7: SAFETY FAILURE AND WITHDRAWAL

If a proposed intervention cannot meet its scientific, legal, ethical, privacy, or safety requirements, it must not proceed.

A future system should provide a documented process for:

Rejecting an unsafe proposal.

Suspending an assessment when critical information is missing.

Reviewing new evidence.

Reporting safety concerns.

Withdrawing authorization when necessary.

Preserving appropriate accountability records.

A system must not claim that an intervention has zero risk or guarantee that all consequences can be reversed.

RULE 8: NO GUARANTEED OUTCOME

A do-over request is a proposed objective, not a guarantee of a particular result.

Future researchers must distinguish between:

The original event.

The requested alternative outcome.

The predicted consequences.

The scientifically demonstrated results, if any.

The possibility of alternate outcomes, historical convergence, or changes in causal relationships remains a research question.

No person should be promised that a proposed intervention will restore a previous life circumstance or produce an exact alternative history.

FINAL DIRECTIVE

The purpose of these rules is to preserve the possibility of responsible future research while establishing strong protections against misuse.

The original goal of a limited personal do-over is retained, but it must never take priority over the safety, rights, privacy, and autonomy of other people.

Non-interference remains the default.

Any future exception would require demonstrated scientific feasibility, independent review, informed consent, legal authorization, and an acceptable assessment of potential consequences.

A requested do-over is not permission to change history. It is a research objective that would first have to satisfy the highest applicable standards of safety, accountability, and respect for others.
