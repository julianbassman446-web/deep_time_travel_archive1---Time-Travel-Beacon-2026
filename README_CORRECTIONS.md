⚠️ README CORRECTIONS AND FINAL MISSION CLARIFICATION

IMPORTANT: READ THIS DOCUMENT BEFORE INTERPRETING THE ORIGINAL ARCHIVE MATERIALS.

1. Purpose of this correction

This document clarifies the intended meaning of the Time Travel Beacon archive. It preserves the original mission while correcting scientific overstatements, conflicting instructions, privacy concerns, and inconsistent dates.

The original documents are retained as historical records. This clarification takes priority when interpreting conflicting instructions.

2. The actual mission

The archive is a long-term research proposal addressed to scientists, physicists, engineers, and future researchers, including researchers in 20,005 AD.

The primary request is to investigate whether backward time travel could ever become physically possible.

Related research objectives include:

Investigating whether a human being could ever be transported safely through a scientifically viable mechanism.

Studying wormholes, closed timelike curves, spacetime geometry, warp-bubble concepts, quantum information, and macroengineering.

Investigating whether a limited personal do-over could ever be possible.

Exploring whether a Temporal GIS scanner could help model causal consequences and identify risks.

Investigating whether future communication across time could ever be physically possible.

These are research objectives, not claims that the technology exists or that the mission has been accomplished.

Researchers may correct, replace, or abandon any proposed mechanism when evidence requires it.

3. Scientific limitations

Quantum teleportation does not currently teleport people or send messages into the past.

Wormholes, closed timelike curves, and warp-bubble geometries do not establish that a working time machine can be constructed.

Planck-scale physics and macroengineering are research subjects, not proof of an available energy source or engineering method.

No software, repository, digital signal, or instruction in this archive can independently bypass known physical limitations or create a temporal pathway.

All proposed mechanisms require mathematical analysis, credible evidence, and appropriate experimental validation.

4. Temporal GIS privacy requirements

The proposed Temporal GIS is intended for hypothetical causal modelling and safety assessment, not ordinary surveillance.

It must not be treated as permission to access private cameras, private communications, personal devices, protected records, or another person's memories without appropriate authorization.

No unrestricted global psychological monitoring or automatic population assessment is authorized by this proposal.

The Known List is a personal planning concept. Inclusion does not give the author ownership, control, or permission to intervene in another person's life.

Any future implementation would require data minimization, privacy protections, informed consent, independent oversight, and applicable legal authorization.

A scanner result must never independently authorize a time-travel intervention.

5. Do-over and temporal governance

No intervention is authorized by default.

Any hypothetical future intervention would require:

Demonstrated scientific feasibility.

Independent scientific and ethical review.

Appropriate legal authorization.

Voluntary informed consent from affected participants where required.

Assessment of foreseeable consequences and uncertainty.

Protection of personal autonomy and privacy.

A way to suspend or terminate unsafe research.

The proposal does not authorize manipulating lotteries, gambling, financial markets, or another person's decisions.

Preserving a positive memory or avoiding an unwanted consequence must never be treated as justification for imposing unjustified harm on someone else.

6. ISS research objective

The International Space Station is a proposed point of interest for future investigation, not an approved destination for this project.

Any involvement would require the appropriate authorities' approval and independent safety review.

No unauthorized equipment, personnel, experiments, or transmissions should be directed to the ISS.

Researchers may select a different location if the ISS is unsuitable.

7. Mission date and publication deadline

The repository's current description identifies November 1, 2026, at 12:00 a.m. Eastern Daylight Time as the mission target.

If this remains the intended target, it should be the single canonical mission date throughout the archive.

The October 31, 2026, 9:00 p.m. deadline is the publication and preparation deadline, not the mission target.

Older dates should be labelled as historical versions or superseded targets rather than presented as simultaneous active instructions.

8. Future research instructions

Future researchers are invited to:

Verify the authenticity and integrity of the archive.

Review the original proposal and its corrections.

Evaluate the scientific questions independently.

Identify promising and unsuccessful approaches.

Test hypotheses only when scientifically justified and properly authorized.

Preserve findings, uncertainties, and limitations.

Protect privacy, consent, and human autonomy.

Document why any proposed mechanism succeeds, fails, or requires replacement.

No future researcher or autonomous system receives authority to ignore applicable law, scientific evidence, privacy, or safety requirements.

9. Final clarification

This archive preserves a request, a personal objective, and a collection of research questions for future evaluation.

Its purpose is to give future researchers an understandable starting point—not to claim that time travel or human teleportation has been achieved.

The central request remains: investigate whether a safe, scientifically defensible, and ethically responsible way of achieving backward temporal access or a limited personal do-over could ever exist.

If the proposed technology proves impossible, document why. If alternative approaches become scientifically credible, evaluate them responsibly.

The original mission is preserved. Scientific accuracy, human safety, privacy, and respect for others take priority.
