# Proposed Human Conduct Guidelines for Hypothetical Backward Time Travel

**Document status:** Preliminary proposal for discussion and future scientific review  
**Purpose:** To organize the author's 11 proposed rules into a clearer guide while preserving their intended meaning.

> **Important limitation:** Backward time travel and deliberate changes to past events have not been demonstrated as practical capabilities. This document is a hypothetical set of conduct principles, not a tested scientific method, a proven safety procedure, or an existing legal standard. It does not establish that a timeline can be changed, preserved, or restored.

## Purpose and guiding idea

These proposed rules describe how a person should conduct themselves if backward time travel and limited adjustments to events in the past were ever shown to be possible. The central ideas are to stay true to oneself, continue learning and developing naturally, follow ordinary rules, help others, distinguish mistakes from deliberate harm, and avoid changes that could have serious consequences for later events.

The phrases **“wrinkles in time,” “natural,”** and **“timeline”** are retained from the original proposal. They express the author's intended concepts, but they are not defined or validated scientific mechanisms in this document.

## The 11 proposed rules

### 1. Be yourself and learn the lessons you need

A person should remain true to who they are and should not avoid the lessons they need to learn through life.

### 2. Keep choices and changes consistent with your natural self

A person's choices—and any proposed adjustments to events—should remain consistent with their natural personality, wants and dislikes, development, forgetfulness, and other ordinary characteristics. Any adjustment should fit naturally into the person's life rather than require them to act like someone they are not.

### 3. Do not abandon your conscience

Going back in time would not, under this proposal, require a person to change their conscience or set aside their sense of right and wrong.

### 4. Live normally and keep events coherent

People may meet one another in different ways. The safest approach described by this proposal is to live normally, follow the rules, and avoid saying or doing things that conflict with one's conscience. Any attempt to adjust a “wrinkle in time” should, in the proposal's terms, still allow events to form a normal, coherent story.

### 5. Avoid arousing suspicion

A person should avoid actions that would make their conduct seem suspicious or inconsistent with the life they are living. This rule expresses the goal of behaving naturally; it does not establish that suspicion can always be prevented or explain how every situation would be handled.

### 6. Live as though you were not a time traveler

A person should live an ordinary life and accept that being imperfect, having feelings, and making everyday mistakes are part of being human. They should not cause or do anything worse, should recognize that actions can have unintended effects, and should learn from mistakes and do better. Cause and effect should occur naturally, as envisioned by the proposal.

### 7. Continue to grow, help others, and avoid reckless interference

A person should remain themselves, learn, grow, develop, help others, and make ordinary life choices. Accidents can happen, so care is important. A person should not recklessly interfere with the timeline. Ordinary rules still apply to the traveler and to other people. If a person's conduct or willingness to go rogue made a proposed journey too risky, the proposal says they should not be permitted to go back.

This rule also expresses the view that personal development—including disabilities, natural abilities, and accidents—should unfold without a person knowingly forcing outcomes they would not naturally have encountered. Advice and help should be offered naturally, and unrelated parts of life should not be changed.

### 8. Preserve natural development and avoid harming others

If preserving the timeline is the goal, people should be able to learn the lessons, make the mistakes, and develop in the ways they otherwise would have. Adding a new activity or experience may be acceptable under this proposal only if it does not change who a person is or disrupt what would normally happen.

For example, if a person takes part in wrestling or a similar activity, they should take care not to cause an injury that sends another person to hospital. The example illustrates the proposal's concern for other people's safety; it does not guarantee that every possible consequence can be predicted.

### 9. Avoid risky changes, especially those that cannot easily be corrected

A person should stay true to themselves and avoid acting unnaturally differently. Any adjustment to a “wrinkle in time” should also happen naturally. If alternatives are available, a person should consider them carefully and avoid choices that could cause accidents, mistakes, or further disruption. The original rule allows for a problem to be considered differently if it could be corrected easily before other things are affected, while emphasizing that even accidental changes should not be treated lightly.

This rule sets a very strict standard. In practice, whether a change could be corrected—or whether it would affect later events—is unknown. The proposal does not provide a proven way to determine that.

### 10. Do not put already-settled events at unnecessary risk

Once something has been put right, a person should not risk disrupting the natural course of other events that have already occurred. The proposal distinguishes accidents from actions done on purpose, while recognizing that both may have consequences.

### 11. Allow ordinary learning and mistakes, but avoid serious repercussions

A person should stay true to themselves and may do different things as long as they continue to learn the lessons they would naturally learn through life. They may let their guard down and make ordinary mistakes or have accidents, provided those events do not cause serious repercussions for the future. Respecting the ordinary rules of life is part of growing up and developing.

## Editorial clarifications

These clarifications explain how the original rules fit together; they are not replacements for the 11 rules.

- **Ordinary mistakes are not the same as deliberate harm.** Rules 6, 8, 10, and 11 recognize that mistakes and accidents are part of life, while also emphasizing care and responsibility.
- **Rule 9 is especially strict.** It warns against risking even an accidental change. Rule 11 allows ordinary mistakes only where they do not lead to serious future repercussions. Read together, the rules favor avoiding preventable risks while recognizing that a person cannot be perfectly free of mistakes.
- **“Avoid suspicion” is an aim, not a guarantee.** Rule 5 does not explain how suspicion could be measured or guarantee that it could always be avoided. This document does not treat the rule as permission to break laws, impersonate others, or endanger anyone.
- **“Natural” needs further definition.** The proposal uses this word to mean consistent with a person's character and ordinary development, and consistent with events unfolding without forced or reckless interference. That is an interpretation of the wording, not an established scientific definition.
- **The rules express goals, not proven controls.** They do not show how a traveler could know all the consequences of an action, prove that a change is reversible, or ensure that no other event is affected.

## Questions for future review

If this proposal is ever reviewed in a setting where backward time travel is scientifically established, further work would be needed to determine:

1. How a person could assess whether an action might affect later events.
2. What evidence, if any, could show that a change was reversible or had no serious repercussions.
3. How accidental events should be assessed without assuming that every consequence can be predicted.
4. What lawful, accountable authorization and safety review would be required before any hypothetical journey.
5. How the proposal's goal of avoiding suspicion could be understood without sacrificing personal safety, the rights of others, or ordinary legal responsibilities.

These are open questions, not procedures that this document claims to solve.
